Free as in… actually free?

Yes. Download DiskSizer and use it without an account or a subscription. The source repository is public; a project license has not yet been selected.

Will it delete anything on its own?

No. Scanning does not change your files. You choose what to remove, and file removal uses the Windows Recycle Bin. A large file is not necessarily safe to delete; previews and paths help you decide. Recycling a file may not immediately free its space.

Why are there two different sizes?

Logical size is the length of a file. On-disk size is its measured allocation. Compression, sparse files, and hardlinks can make those numbers differ. DiskSizer counts known shared identities once in folder allocation totals and marks unknowns. Allocation is not a promise of reclaimable space.

Can I come back to a scan?

Scans save to your computer automatically. Open a saved scan to inspect that dated result, or save a named copy. Saved scans are read-only: rescan for current results and before removing files. Saved scan files use disk space until you remove them.

What do I need to run it?

Windows 10 or 11 on x64. Download the ZIP, extract it, and open DiskSizer.exe. The current build is unsigned, so Windows may show a publisher warning. See the release notes and checksums for the download details.
